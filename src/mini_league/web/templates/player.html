How the rating works

Skill starts at {{ "%.0f"|format(config.mu) }} and moves with every result. Beating someone well above you moves it further than beating someone you were meant to beat. Uncertainty starts at {{ "%.2f"|format(config.sigma) }} and shrinks as you play, so a newcomer swings about and a regular barely moves.

The rating is skill minus {{ "%.0f"|format(config.conservative_k) }} times the uncertainty, times {{ "%.0f"|format(config.display_scale) }}. For {{ player.name }}: ({{ "%.2f"|format(current.mu) }} − {{ "%.0f"|format(config.conservative_k) }} × {{ "%.2f"|format(current.sigma) }}) × {{ "%.0f"|format(config.display_scale) }} = {{ current.rating }}. Subtracting the uncertainty is deliberate: one good morning should not put you top. Only wins and losses count, never the scores.

What the numbers actually mean

Skill is a guess at something nobody can see directly: how much you help a side win. The app never measures it, it only infers it from results, and the number has no units you would feel on the pitch.

Uncertainty is how sure that guess is, and the rating is the two put together, deliberately pessimistically. Read it as “{{ player.name }} is at least this good”, not “{{ player.name }} is this good”. Taking the guess at face value would put {{ player.name }} at {{ mu_rating }} rather than {{ current.rating }}; everyone is marked down the same way, so the order stays fair even though every number is an understatement.

That is why turning up raises your rating even when you win as often as you lose: the uncertainty shrinks. Early in a season most of a climb is the league growing surer of you rather than you getting better, and a player with four games sits low because little is known, not because they are bad.
